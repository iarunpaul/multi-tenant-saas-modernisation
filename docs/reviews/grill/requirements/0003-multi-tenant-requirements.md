## Purpose

Challenge whether the requirements for modernising the database-per-tenant SaaS platform are clear, testable, and current enough to guide detailed architecture and eventual implementation without prematurely prescribing technology.

## Outcome

The requirements describe the intended business outcome and major architecture drivers coherently. The associated ADR now documents a proposed hybrid, sharded-default topology. The requirements remain suitable as directional input for detailed design using explicit prototype assumptions, but they are not a validated production acceptance baseline.

The requirements' phase/status text is stale: it still says no architecture decision has been made and names requirements Grill as the next activity, even though ADR-0001 and multiple architecture reviews now exist. Refresh that status and traceability before using this document as current governance evidence.

## Confirmed Facts

The previous requirements Grill recorded the following as confirmed inputs. This review carries them forward but does not independently validate them against operational data:

- Current estate: approximately 100,000 tenants; planning range of 250,000–300,000 over three to five years.
- Tenant workload distribution is highly skewed.
- Aggregate sustained throughput: 10,000–20,000 TPS; short burst near 30,000 TPS; largest tenants peak at 500–1,000 TPS.
- Interactive API targets: P95 below 300 ms and P99 below 750 ms.
- Availability objectives: 99.95% for standard tenants and 99.99% for higher tiers where justified.
- Independent tenant recovery is required; standard-tenant prototype RPO/RTO are 15 minutes/four hours.
- Migration is incremental by tenant, with online copy/catch-up where practical and a maximum five-minute final write-quiesce target.
- EU/EEA residency is the initial baseline, including primary data, replicas, backups, and recovery copies.
- Prototype deletion and backup assumptions are 30-day active deletion and 35-day standard backup retention.
- Customer-visible backup/restore behavior must be preserved; legacy formats need a transitional import/recovery path.

## Accepted Assumptions

- Quantitative targets are prototype/planning inputs, not validated capacity limits or customer-facing SLOs.
- Workload remains skewed, and exceptional tenants may need throttling, promotion, or dedicated placement.
- EU/EEA is sufficient for the initial prototype; other jurisdictions and contractual regimes require later validation.
- Higher service tiers may require stronger recovery objectives.
- External API and integration contracts are expected to remain compatible, but a complete inventory and verification evidence are not yet recorded.
- ADR-0001's hybrid topology is a proposed direction; authorized decision-owner acceptance is not established by the ADR's current status.

## What Looks Sound

- The requirements separate business outcomes and quality attributes from technology selection.
- Tenant isolation is end-to-end, including identity, authorization, persistence, caches, messaging, background jobs, telemetry, backup/restore, and administration.
- Workload skew, tenant movement, noisy neighbors, and differentiated placement are treated as material.
- Migration includes validation, reconciliation, partial failure, rollback/recovery, and operational visibility.
- Recovery requirements include deletion-state preservation and geographic constraints.
- The document rejects distributed-system patterns without a specific driver.
- The earlier Grill established a working point-of-no-return rule for migration, sufficient to proceed to architecture option analysis while leaving implementation-level fencing and reconciliation for detailed design.

## Critical Challenges

### 1. Requirements status and decision traceability are stale

**Issue:** The status section says architecture has not been decided and requirements Grill is next, while ADR-0001 proposes a hybrid sharded-default topology and has been reviewed.  
**Why it matters:** Readers cannot distinguish current requirements, proposed decisions, and accepted decisions; implementation teams may treat a proposal as approved or repeat completed work.  
**Question or evidence required:** Update phase, status, next activity, and links to ADR/review evidence; state explicitly that the topology remains proposed pending authorized acceptance.  
**Potential consequence:** Governance and implementation may proceed against a mistaken decision state.

### 2. Quantitative targets are not yet acceptance criteria

**Issue:** Precise scale, throughput, latency, availability, recovery, and migration figures are recorded without provenance, accountable owners, workload definition, measurement windows, or a clear distinction between design objectives and contractual service commitments.  
**Why it matters:** The figures cannot reliably drive shard sizing, capacity planning, or production acceptance without transaction mix, concurrency, burst duration, tenant distribution, and measurement methodology.  
**Question or evidence required:** For each metric, record source/baseline, owner, workload and measurement method, and whether it is a prototype objective, launch criterion, or contractual SLO.  
**Potential consequence:** The architecture may be sized or approved against numbers that are not representative or agreed.

### 3. Tenant isolation needs verifiable acceptance evidence

**Issue:** Isolation principles cover the right system surfaces, but acceptance criteria for demonstrating prevention of cross-tenant access—including privileged support and administrative access—are not specified.  
**Why it matters:** Shared-schema tenancy increases the consequences of omitted tenant scoping; written principles alone do not demonstrate enforcement.  
**Question or evidence required:** Define required negative and boundary tests for tenant data access, cache, messages, background jobs, restore, and privileged operations; leave the specific implementation controls to detailed design.  
**Potential consequence:** Production could proceed without objective evidence for a core security constraint.

### 4. Regulatory and contractual scope needs an owner and gate

**Issue:** EU/EEA is identified as the prototype baseline, while applicable customer-specific regimes and obligations remain to be confirmed.  
**Why it matters:** Requirements may govern replicas, backup copies, disaster recovery, support access, retention, and tenant movement, changing placement and recovery decisions.  
**Question or evidence required:** Identify who validates legal/contractual scope and what evidence is required before enabling each region or tenant cohort.  
**Potential consequence:** Region placement that follows the prototype assumption may still breach an applicable obligation.

### 5. Backup compatibility and deletion obligations are not bounded

**Issue:** The requirements preserve customer-visible recovery and require transitional legacy-backup import, but do not bound supported format versions, import acceptance, support duration, or deletion handling through backup expiry.  
**Why it matters:** Restores may fail for legacy customers or reintroduce deleted data; indefinite compatibility also creates a permanent support and security burden.  
**Question or evidence required:** Define the compatibility window, integrity criteria, deletion replay evidence, retention exceptions, and retirement owner before migration rollout.  
**Potential consequence:** Recovery commitments or deletion requirements may not be met.

## Hidden Assumptions

- Tenant and workload projections remain representative over the planning horizon.
- Aggregate peak load and the largest per-tenant peaks have a defined relationship and can be tested together.
- A five-minute quiesce target is feasible for large tenants and high change rates.
- Customer-visible restore behavior can be preserved even when internal backup formats change.
- Cost and operational-reduction goals can be measured consistently against a current-state baseline.
- “Dedicated shard,” “dedicated database,” and “dedicated infrastructure” are distinct, governed placement tiers.

## Missing Non-Functional Requirements

- A reproducible workload and measurement profile for throughput, latency, burst, concurrency, and noisy-neighbor acceptance.
- Per-shard capacity and maximum tenant/workload blast-radius limits.
- Tenant-directory availability, recovery, and stale-routing objectives.
- Measurable tenant-recovery acceptance criteria for isolation, deletion, residency, RPO, and RTO.
- Isolation test scope, privileged-access controls, and required automated evidence.
- Ownership and measurement windows for availability and service-tier objectives.
- Tenant-identifying telemetry access, retention, redaction, and cardinality requirements.
- Baseline and calculation method for cost, provisioning, schema rollout, and manual-intervention goals.

## Failure Scenarios

- Stale placement directs requests or background jobs to the prior shard during tenant movement.
- A hot tenant exhausts shared capacity and harms unrelated tenants.
- Migration copy/catch-up omits, duplicates, or reorders writes.
- Old workers continue writing to the legacy store after target activation.
- Tenant restore changes neighboring data or resurrects post-backup deletions.
- Replicas, backups, or recovery copies violate a tenant's permitted geography.
- Privileged support access bypasses tenant-context controls.
- Directory or regional failure prevents tenant resolution or recovery within objectives.

## Complexity Assessment

```text
appropriately simple
```

The requirements justify comparing shared/sharded and dedicated placement because tenant scale, workload skew, isolation, residency, and recovery constrain the solution. They do not justify microservices, Kubernetes, event sourcing, CQRS, distributed transactions, or active-active multi-region operation without additional evidence.

## Recommendation

```text
PROCEED WITH CONDITIONS
```

The requirements provide sufficient directional input for detailed design using the explicitly recorded prototype assumptions. They are not yet a production acceptance contract. Refresh the stale status and resolve the significant items at their design or rollout gates; do not present unvalidated figures as production capacity or service commitments.

## Conditions

1. Update the requirements phase/status, next activity, and decision traceability to reflect ADR-0001 as proposed pending authorized acceptance.
2. Preserve prototype values as assumptions until their sources, owners, workload profiles, and measurement methods are established.
3. Define and verify isolation and recovery proof obligations before production implementation or adoption.
4. Confirm applicable regulatory and contractual scope before onboarding tenants in each region.
5. Bound legacy-backup support and verify deletion-safe restore behavior before migration rollout.
6. Establish current-state baselines before claiming cost or operational-effort reductions.

## Resolved During Grill

- Prior reviews recorded working tenant scale/workload figures, availability and recovery targets, EU/EEA prototype scope, deletion/backup assumptions, and migration downtime target.
- The prior Grill established the working migration rule: rollback is permitted before target writes; after the target accepts a committed write, recovery must reconcile or move forward rather than blindly route back.
- These values and rules are inherited review context and have not been independently validated against production evidence in this review.

## Remaining Follow-Up

- Confirm source data and accountable owners for scale, workload, latency, availability, and recovery targets.
- Define representative workload acceptance, shard limits, and placement thresholds during detailed architecture design.
- Specify directory behavior, migration fencing/consistency, shared-schema isolation enforcement, and tenant recovery in the relevant detailed decisions.
- Confirm regional/customer obligations and legacy-backup support boundaries before production rollout.
